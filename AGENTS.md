# AGENTS

- Two sources, and they are different things. **The protocol** is what any client and any world must agree on: the nine documents in `../Nostr-Spatial-Web-Protocol--NSWP-/protocol/` (index: `README.md` there; online at <https://github.com/PR0M3TH3AN/Nostr-Spatial-Web-Protocol--NSWP-/tree/main/protocol>), each with a schema and vectors. **BitPortal's own design** is `../Nostr-Spatial-Web-Protocol--NSWP-/spec.md`, Part II and §20. BitPortal is one client of that protocol and must keep a working no-BitBlocks mode (`?plain=1`).
- Nothing another client or world would have to copy may live only in this repo. If a change here alters what goes on the wire (an event, the arrival context, a look, a room message, how a list is read), change the protocol document, its schema and its vectors in the NSWP repo first, then vendor it here.
- Before finishing a change: `npm test`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run vendor:check`, `npm run previews:check`, `npm run build`.
- **Saved doors and the room (spec §14.1):** saved places (`src/shared/saved.ts`) and the room's layout (`src/shared/room.ts`) stay in this browser. Never publish them or send them anywhere without the owner's decision. A saved door keeps its place on the wall for as long as it is saved; don't reorder the wall. The room draws built-in furniture only: nothing from a layout may cause a request to another site.
- **Going by name (NSWP-10):** the Airlock may sign a pass only when the visitor chose "by name" on that screen, for a verified world, with a session key made for that trip (`src/shared/pass.ts`). It uses BitLogin's headless signer (`quietSigner` in `src/shared/session.ts`), which can restore a session and can't start one. Never mount `<bitlogin-auth>` or any sign-in form on `/go/`: a sign-in prompt mid-crossing is what a fake Airlock shows (spec §6.3, §12.3). Never make "by name" the default, widen it to unverified worlds, lengthen a pass, or reuse a session key without the owner's decision. `?signer=test` on `/go/` and `/publish/` signs with a throwaway key and is the only way automation may exercise signing.
- **Panels:** a visitor's panels, their library and any file in it (`bitportal:panels` and `bitportal:panels:library` in localStorage, files in IndexedDB) stay in this browser, like saved doors. What is typed on the room's keyboard goes nowhere but the note it was typed for. Never upload, publish or share them without the owner's decision. Don't loosen the CSP to let a panel load pictures or video from other sites: that tells those sites who is looking. A panel never shows a web page.
- **Your computers (NSWP `browser/bridge.md`):** the room can open a terminal on a computer of the visitor's own (`src/walk/computers.ts`, `src/shared/computers.ts`). It runs commands on their machine, so: a computer is added only by the visitor pressing the button on the sheet that shows its whole key, never by a link alone and never in a headset where the sheet can't be read; the list (`bitportal:computers`) stays in this browser; the signer is BitLogin's headless one and is asked for a grant and nothing else; the terminal's code is loaded only when one is opened. Never without the owner's decision: add a computer without that press, keep or send what is typed, sign anything for the Bridge but a grant, offer any of this to a world or on `/go/`, or accept a `ws://` relay. The Bridge asks at the computer; nothing here may try to answer for the visitor. How large a visitor likes a terminal's letters (`bitportal:terminal-letters`) stays in this browser too, and which terminals were open (`bitportal:terminals-open`, for the tab, so a refresh can bring them back) is kept nowhere else. Speech is the kit's (`nswp-sdk`): it goes to the visitor's own computer, or to their browser's recogniser after they are told; never add a speech service of ours or anyone's. Automation uses `__bitportalTest.computers.useTestIdentity()`, which makes a throwaway key in the page and can't be switched on by an address, with a Bridge and a relay on the test machine.
- **Worlds the visitor is building (`src/shared/my-worlds.ts`, `bringTo` in `src/walk/computers.ts`):** the one case where a world is handed anything to do with the visitor's computer, by the owner's decision of 2026-11-18. A world gets on the list only by the visitor typing its address on the sheet and pressing the button, on a flat screen; the list (`bitportal:my-worlds`) stays in this browser. Going there, the room signs a world grant for that origin alone and hands it over after the `#` of that world's own address. Never without the owner's decision: add a world to the list any other way (a link, a door, a world asking, a default), hand a world grant to an origin that isn't on it, send it through `/go/` or put it in the arrival context, lengthen it, or give a world the signer. This does not go through the Airlock, and nothing about it may be added to the Airlock.
- **A home of the visitor's own (spec §14.1, NSWP-07; `src/shared/home.ts`):** a visitor may choose a world as where they start, in Settings and nowhere else. It is allowed only when every check in `checkHome` holds: an https address, their own announcement of it, its site admitting their key, and its saying it can be a home. Never without the owner's decision: drop or soften a check, let a link, a door or a world set or suggest a home, take the site's word alone or the announcement alone as proof it is theirs, or go to a home without the Airlock looking at it first. BitPortal's room is always the fallback and always where doors, sign-in and computers live; nothing may leave a visitor with nowhere to start. `doors=1` always leads to the room, whatever their home is.
- **Blocks that travel (NSWP-02 "Blocks that travel", `src/shared/blocked.ts`):** the people a visitor has blocked everywhere are a list in this browser (`bitportal:blocked`; `bitportal:blocked-pending` for reports not yet answered). The Airlock hands the keys to each world they arrive in. Never without the owner's decision: add anyone to the list without the visitor seeing who and ticking the box (it starts unticked), use the fast path past that question, take anyone off because a link said so, publish the list or send it anywhere but a world's arrival context, send it to a site that hasn't adopted the protocol, or raise its limits. On the way home nothing is asked, so a report waits. Settings is where the list is undone, and it says that each world arrived in learns the keys.
- **Staying joined (NSWP-02 "Staying joined", `src/shared/travel.ts`):** a link's `joined=1` and `mic=1` are a claim anyone can write. The Airlock shows them and the visitor decides: "stay in the room" is ticked by default and theirs to untick; "keep my microphone on between worlds" is off until they tick it, and remembered in this browser (`bitportal:travel-mic`). Never without the owner's decision: pass `mic` on without that tick, default it to on, pass either to a site that hasn't adopted the protocol, or pass a microphone without joined. On the way home nothing is asked, so a microphone goes only when the tick was already given. The Airlock still asks one thing, whether to go: these are what goes along, like the look.
- **Music shared in the room (`sharedMusic` in `src/shared/instance.ts`):** a visitor may offer the music they are playing to people near them, and others hear it only if they turned that on. Whether this copy's room has it at all is the host's answer. Never turn hearing or sharing on for a visitor, and don't change `sharedMusic` without the owner's decision.
- **Sound in the room (the kit's `createSounds`; `bitportal:sound`, `bitportal:jukebox`, songs in IndexedDB):** the room has the kit's recorded cues and hum (`vendor/nswp/sounds/`, served at `/sounds/kit/`), eight pieces of music of its own (`public/sounds/room-*.ogg`, played in turn), and a Sound menu. Every one of those files was chosen by the owner by ear: never add, swap or remove one without their hearing it, and never add one whose terms for redistribution are unclear unless the owner has settled them, as they did for YuE2 on 2026-11-18 (`public/sounds/README.md`). A visitor's volumes and their own songs stay in this browser, like saved doors; only they hear their songs. Never upload, publish or share a song, fetch a sound from another site, add sound files or a remote player to BitPortal, play anything before the visitor's first touch, or let a world's sounds follow a visitor into BitPortal's own places.
- **A shared screen in the room (the kit's `addRoomControls`, NSWP `browser/spec.md` A4):** someone at a desk can offer their screen to the room. It is sent only to people who choose to watch, on a panel of the watcher's own. Never open anyone's screen unasked, send it to everyone, record it, or add sound to it without the owner's decision.
- **Casual and intense in the room (the kit's `createFps` and `createXrInput`; `bitportal:fps`):** two ways of moving, with the same names at a desk and in a headset. Intense at a desk: the mouse looks, keys walk, Shift runs, F uses, Escape is the menu. Intense in a headset: walking and turning smoothly. Casual until the visitor chooses, each device by itself, kept in this browser, and carried through the Airlock as a preference (`moving`), with nothing asked. Never make intense the default, let a choice at a desk change how a headset moves, take the pointer before the visitor has pressed anything, or hang anything else on that flag, without the owner's decision. Arriving already moving the intense way, the mouse looks at once and the pointer is taken at their first key or click, with no click asked for: the owner's decision of 2026-11-18. Running and jumping are in both ways of moving and are the kit's own (`RUN`, `addJump`): the room adds neither a speed nor a jump of its own.
- **Signs in the room:** on a flat screen, what a door is goes in the card over the buttons (`controls.setTarget(name, detail)`), never on a sign hung in front of it. The sign in the world is a headset's, low on the door, and kept the same size in the visitor's view (`viewScale`). Don't add a sign of fixed size that a visitor can walk up to.
- **Moving in the room:** on a flat screen a tap or click is a walk at the keys' pace, three metres at most (the kit's `createStroll`). In a headset a step stays a jump. Don't change either without the owner's decision.
- **Local copies on a development server (the kit's `toLocal` and `fromLocal`, NSWP `docs/local-server.md`):** served from a developer's machine, the Airlock sends the visitor to the local copy of a site that is being served there, and gives worlds its own real address for the way back. Only the last step changes. Everything the Airlock checks, shows as the destination, pins and remembers is the real address, and it says on the card when the visit is to a copy (`#local-copy`). A pass is made for the place it is shown at, so there it is made for the copy. Never use the map for a check, for `catalog.permits`, or for anything to do with a computer: `bringTo` is for a world's real address alone. A deployed BitPortal asks for no map; don't add a setting or an address that makes it.
- **The visitor's console and their form (NSWP-11):** the room keeps the summons like any world: the key left of 1, two fingers held, B or Y held, and nothing in the room is ever bound to those. The Airlock passes a visitor's form (`form`) to the world they are going to: how they pressed the Airlock's own page when that was seen, and otherwise what the link said. It is a layout preference and nothing else: never show it, ask about it, or decide anything by it, and never send it to a site that hasn't adopted the protocol.
- **Through a door in the room:** a door chosen from where the visitor stands (a click, a trigger, the list) brings the crossing's room up; a doorway walked into does not, and they go at once to the Airlock. The Airlock still asks its one question either way. The room's music is named in the Sound section by its file (`room-a` to `room-h`): the owner points pieces out by those names, so never rename one without saying so.
- **The list in the room (`src/walk/door-list.ts`):** every door this copy shows, on a panel in the room. The "List" control and the "more on the list" button on a wall open it; neither leaves the room. It is one more way to a world, so every door on it has passed `catalog.permits`, and choosing one is choosing a door: the crossing comes up and the Airlock asks. Never send a visitor from the room to a page to read a list, and never put on the panel a door the walls and the front page wouldn't show. Its buttons are at its top, clear of the row of controls.
- **The way home leads to the room (`/go/` with no destination):** a visitor coming home from a world lands in the room (`/walk/#back`), whichever way they left: by one of its doors, from the list, or by a link from somewhere else. They are coming from a 3D place, perhaps in a headset, and the list is a page of text; it is one press away in the room. Never send the way home to the list. A visitor with a home of their own goes there first, and the room is the fallback (see "A home of the visitor's own").
- **Crossings (NSWP-02, "How many actions a crossing takes"):** the Airlock asks one thing, whether to go. Anything else on it must arrive already chosen and never need a second press; don't add steps, interstitials or confirmations to `/go/` or to leaving the room. The chamber in the room is scenery drawn by the page: never put anything to press in it, and never let it say a destination is verified. `xr=1` on the way in becomes `immersive` on the way out, and nothing else about the visitor's device is passed on.
- **Hands in a headset:** the room reads controllers and tracked hands through the NSWP kit's input layer (`createXrInput`), which knows no make or model. Don't add controller-specific button code here: a gap goes back to the kit (`packages/sdk/src/actions.ts`, which has tests). Never fetch a controller's or a hand's model from another site, and never make a stick, a grip or a button the only way to do something: every action in the room stays one `select`.
- **What this copy shows (`src/shared/instance.ts`, `src/shared/catalog.ts`, spec §15):** the host's answers bind every visitor. Never without the owner's decision: add or change an operator key, turn `allowListOnly` off, turn `allowAdult` on, add a setting, link or address that lets a visitor get round either, or let a list a visitor follows or an unlisted world through on a strict copy. Every way to a world asks the same question: the lobby, the room, saved doors, search, a hashtag and the Airlock. A new way to a world goes through `catalog.permits` or it isn't done. A world for adults is never shown or gone to here, listed or not, and `audienceOf` (a warning with no audience is adult) is the only reading of a rating. The Airlock still shows the address it won't go to.
- **The operator's list (`src/shared/operator.ts`):** only the operator, signed in with a key in `operators`, sees what is waiting or can list. Listing signs and publishes to public relays, so it happens only when the operator presses the button themself: never from automation, a test, a link or a setting, and never with any key but a throwaway in a test. Tests swap the operator's key for a throwaway and answer every relay themselves.
- **Hashtags (`src/shared/tastes.ts`):** what a visitor asked for more or less of stays in this browser (`bitportal:hashtags`). Never publish it or ask a relay a question that gives it away without the owner's decision. "Less" moves worlds down and never hides them: hiding belongs to the host's answers above. A world that ranks higher for a hashtag says which.
- **Lists (NSWP-08):** which lists a visitor follows (`src/shared/lists.ts`) stays in this browser, like saved doors. Never publish it or send it anywhere without the owner's decision. A followed list changes only when the same key signs a later copy. Worlds no list names are shown only when the visitor asks, apart from listed worlds, under the limits in `src/shared/catalog.ts` (`UNLISTED_LIMITS`); don't raise them or show those worlds unasked without the owner's decision. What goes on BitPortal's own list is the owner's decision each time.
- **Lobby privacy (spec §5.7):** listing a world must not contact its origin. Only opening a door or the Airlock may. No remote images, fonts or scripts; the CSP enforces same-origin for all three.
- **Previews (spec §5.8):** a door shows a preview only from BitPortal's own mirror (`public/previews/`, filled by `npm run previews`), and only after the bytes match the hash in the signed announcement. Anything not mirrored waits until the door is opened. All of that is decided in `src/shared/preview.ts`; the list and the hall must not fetch a preview any other way. Never show one unasked for a world with a content warning or a world outside the visitor's chosen lists.
- Vendored code under `vendor/` and `public/fonts/` is generated by `npm run vendor` from clean sibling checkouts (`-- --only <name>` takes one and leaves the rest as recorded; never vendor from a checkout with uncommitted changes, and never clean someone else's checkout to make it so). Never edit it by hand. Gaps found in a block go back to that block's repo, not into a local patch.
- BitBlocks conventions apply: lowercase hex `pubkey` (D1), `--bitportal-*` CSS properties (D3), snake_case error codes (D4), one version number (D6).
- **Sign-in:** BitLogin is the only session source. Pass its signer to code explicitly (`src/shared/session.ts`); never read `window.nostr` elsewhere. Check every signature a signer returns. `<bitlogin-auth>` must stay in one fixed place in the page: moving or removing it ends its worker. Never create or sign in to a BitLogin account from automation; use `/publish/?signer=test`.
- Never commit keys. The curator key is at `~/.config/nswp/bitportal-curator.key`.
- Deploying, publishing events to relays and changing the starter set's contents are outward-facing: the repo owner approves each.
- **The visitor's look (spec §4.2):** how others see them (`src/shared/look.ts`, edited at `/look/`). It stays in this browser. Nothing is kept until the visitor makes one. It leaves only through the Airlock, in the arrival context, and only when the visitor chose "yourself" on that trip; a site that hasn't adopted the protocol is never sent it. Never publish it or send it anywhere else without the owner's decision. (This is a different thing from the next rule, which is about the site's own appearance.)
- **Look:** dark only. One colour of light, `--bitportal-accent`; the worlds supply every other colour through their doors. Three shapes: the arch is a door, the block (a notched corner, a small square) is a bit, the grid is the space. Panels and buttons are square or notched, never rounded. The Airlock stays plain: nothing there may compete with the address. The 3D sky, ground, furniture and the doorway's modelled frame come from the SDK (`nswp-sdk/home`; see the NSWP repo's `docs/grid-look.md`), shared with the reference worlds, so change them there; a 3D door is that frame round a view, in `src/shared/door3d.ts`. Model anything new with the SDK's kit, not plain boxes. The landing page's room (`src/lobby/hero.ts`) must draw from the same data as the list and contact nothing the list doesn't, and the page must be whole without it.
- UI copy: plain language, sentence case, say what a button does. The domain a door leads to is always shown in full, in the monospace face.
