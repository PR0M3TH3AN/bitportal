# AGENTS

- Two sources, and they are different things. **The protocol** is what any client and any world must agree on: the eleven documents in `../Nostr-Spatial-Web-Protocol--NSWP-/protocol/` (index: `README.md` there; online at <https://github.com/PR0M3TH3AN/Nostr-Spatial-Web-Protocol--NSWP-/tree/main/protocol>), each with a schema and vectors. **BitPortal's own design** is `../Nostr-Spatial-Web-Protocol--NSWP-/spec.md`, Part II and §20. BitPortal is one client of that protocol and must keep a working no-BitBlocks mode (`?plain=1`).
- Nothing another client or world would have to copy may live only in this repo. If a change here alters what goes on the wire (an event, the arrival context, a look, a room message, how a list is read), change the protocol document, its schema and its vectors in the NSWP repo first, then vendor it here.
- Before finishing a change: `npm run gate`. It is every check this repository has (`vendor:check`, `previews:check`, `typecheck`, `test`, `build`), run on this machine, stopping at the first that fails with its exit code. **Nothing is merged on a gate that didn't exit 0**, and whoever asks for a merge says in the pull request that they ran it and how many tests passed. There is no check run anywhere else (the owner, 2026-11-21: "We should do the CI tests local"). A test that fails only while the machine is busy still failed: run it again when it is quiet, and say so. A new check goes into `scripts/gate.mjs`, or it isn't one.
- **Saved doors and the room (spec §14.1):** saved places (`src/shared/saved.ts`) and the room's layout (`src/shared/room.ts`) stay in this browser. Never publish them or send them anywhere without the owner's decision. A saved door keeps its place on the wall for as long as it is saved; don't reorder the wall. The room draws built-in furniture only: nothing from a layout may cause a request to another site.
- **Going by name (NSWP-10):** this is how a copy that names no signer behaves: while `INSTANCE.signer` is set, as it is here, the Airlock signs no pass and offers no "by name" ("No pass on arrival", below). On a copy that names none, the Airlock may sign a pass only when the visitor chose "by name" on that screen, for a verified world, with a session key made for that trip (`src/shared/pass.ts`). It asks the sign-in address's signer through `quietSigner` in `src/shared/session.ts`, which signs for someone already signed in and can't sign anyone in. Never put a sign-in form, a sign-in button or a window that opens by itself on `/go/`: a sign-in prompt mid-crossing is what a fake Airlock shows (spec §6.3, §12.3). "By name" is the default only for someone signed in, and only on a copy that names no signer ("Signed in everywhere", below). Never widen it to unverified worlds, lengthen a pass, or reuse a session key without the owner's decision. `?signer=test` on `/go/` and `/publish/` signs with a throwaway key and is the only way automation may exercise signing.
- **Panels:** a visitor's panels, their library and any file in it (`bitportal:panels` and `bitportal:panels:library` in localStorage, files in IndexedDB) stay in this browser, like saved doors. What is typed on the room's keyboard goes nowhere but the note it was typed for, or, when it was put up to write to the room, to the people in that room (NSWP-06 "Words"). Never upload, publish or share them without the owner's decision. Don't loosen the CSP to let a panel load pictures or video from other sites: that tells those sites who is looking. A panel never shows a web page.
- **Your computers (NSWP `browser/bridge.md`):** the room can open a terminal on a computer of the visitor's own (`src/walk/computers.ts`, `src/shared/computers.ts`). It runs commands on their machine, so: a computer is added only by the visitor pressing the button on the sheet that shows its whole key, never by a link alone and never in a headset where the sheet can't be read; the list (`bitportal:computers`) stays in this browser; the signer is the sign-in address's (`quietSigner`), is asked for a grant and nothing else, and signs one only at the address BitLogin reserves grants to (`holdsReserved`); the terminal's code is loaded only when one is opened. Never without the owner's decision: add a computer without that press, keep or send what is typed, sign anything for the Bridge but a grant, offer any of this to a world or on `/go/`, or accept a `ws://` relay. The Bridge asks at the computer; nothing here may try to answer for the visitor. How large a visitor likes a terminal's letters (`bitportal:terminal-letters`) stays in this browser too, and which terminals were open (`bitportal:terminals-open`, for the tab, so a refresh can bring them back) is kept nowhere else. Speech is the kit's (`nswp-sdk`): it goes to the visitor's own computer, or to their browser's recogniser after they are told; never add a speech service of ours or anyone's. Automation uses `__bitportalTest.computers.useTestIdentity()`, which makes a throwaway key in the page and can't be switched on by an address, with a Bridge and a relay on the test machine.
- **Worlds the visitor is building (`src/shared/my-worlds.ts`, `bringTo` in `src/walk/computers.ts`):** the one case where a world is handed anything to do with the visitor's computer, by the owner's decision of 2026-11-18. A world gets on the list only by the visitor typing its address on the sheet and pressing the button, on a flat screen; the list (`bitportal:my-worlds`) stays in this browser. Going there, the room signs a world grant for that origin alone and hands it over after the `#` of that world's own address. Never without the owner's decision: add a world to the list any other way (a link, a door, a world asking, a default), hand a world grant to an origin that isn't on it, send it through `/go/` or put it in the arrival context, lengthen it, or give a world the signer. This does not go through the Airlock, and nothing about it may be added to the Airlock.
- **A home of the visitor's own (spec §14.1, NSWP-07; `src/shared/home.ts`):** a visitor may choose a world as where they start, in Settings and nowhere else. It is allowed only when every check in `checkHome` holds: an https address, their own announcement of it, its site admitting their key, and its saying it can be a home. Never without the owner's decision: drop or soften a check, let a link, a door or a world set or suggest a home, take the site's word alone or the announcement alone as proof it is theirs, or go to a home without the Airlock looking at it first. BitPortal's room is always the fallback and always where doors, sign-in and computers live; nothing may leave a visitor with nowhere to start. `doors=1` always leads to the room, whatever their home is.
- **Blocks that travel (NSWP-02 "Blocks that travel", `src/shared/blocked.ts`):** the people a visitor has blocked everywhere are a list in this browser (`bitportal:blocked`; `bitportal:blocked-pending` for reports not yet answered). Never without the owner's decision: add anyone to the list without the visitor seeing who and ticking the box (it starts unticked), use the fast path past that question, take anyone off because a link said so, publish the list or send it anywhere but a world's arrival context, send it to a site that hasn't adopted the protocol, or raise its limits. On the way home nothing is asked, so a report waits. Settings is where the list is undone, and it says that each world arrived in learns the keys.

On the way home nothing is asked, so a microphone goes only when the tick was already given. The Airlock still asks one thing, whether to go: these are what goes along, like the look.

  - The room asks through `session.ts`'s `usePanel` hook with the kit's words (`askClientForSignIn`), and learns who is signed in from the signer, as always: the panel only says when to look. `allowed` from the panel is a hint for not waiting on a no, never a sign-in.
  - **"Your purchases" is the same panel again** (`mode: "library"`): the receipts BitLogin keeps for the visitor. BitPortal sees none of them. **The room offers a tip to a person** (`tipPerson` in `session.ts`, given to the kit's room controls as `tip`): it hands BitLogin's application client a key and an amount (`client.tip`, BitLogin `docs/paying.md`) and nothing else; BitLogin's own page finds where that person is paid, says who it is for, and pays from the visitor's wallet at their press. BitPortal names no invoice and no address, takes no share, and gates nothing on the answer. **Never to the room itself** (`tipsWorld: false`): the room is no world and names no address to be tipped at; don't add one without the owner. Don't widen `notes.propose` to another kind without the owner. BitLogin's client asking the person has not been run against a real sign-in from here (no account is made by automation): only the room's side of it, with a throwaway identity.

- **One sign-in address (`src/shared/session.ts`; owner's decision of 2026-11-19, after a world signed them in as a different account from their home room):** a visitor signs in to BitPortal at the same address a world sends them to (`INSTANCE.signer`, BitLogin's own site), in that address's own window or, inside `/shell/`, its own panel (above). BitPortal is an application to BitLogin's signer (BitLogin `docs/sign-in-window.md` §SW9): it loads `bitlogin-client.js`, which embeds the signer in an unseen frame, says what BitPortal asks for, and signs only what the visitor allowed in the window. So: no page of BitPortal has a sign-in form, a password field, `<bitlogin-auth>`, BitLogin's widget script or its headless signer (a test pins it); BitPortal holds no key and no session of its own; and it runs no WebAssembly. `permissionsFor` is everything every visitor is asked to allow (a post, a pass, two kinds of grant, the operator's list, the settings note under `bitportal/`, a world announcement): adding a kind, a prefix, a proof or a peer to it is the owner's decision, and nothing sensitive (a profile, follows, deletions, relay or mute lists, messages) is ever added. Passes and grants (27300, 27302, 27303) are signed by BitLogin only where it says it will: BitPortal's own address, and a local copy of BitPortal beside a local copy of BitLogin. BitPortal asks for everything first and reads the answer (`canAsk`); where it is turned down it asks again without those three, so signing in still works and the room is joined as a guest with no terminal. `NAMED_ADDRESSES` is gone by only for a sign-in address too old to say, or silent. Never decide it by an address where the sign-in address can be asked, and never ask for less than everything first. The content policy names the sign-in address in `frame-src` and nothing else, read from `INSTANCE.signer` by the build; `frame-ancestors 'none'` stays. The Airlock loads nothing from the sign-in address: it reads `bitportal:signed-in`, this browser's own note of who the signer last said was signed in (a public key, a name and when it runs out; never synced, and nothing is signed on its word).
